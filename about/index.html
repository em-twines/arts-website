Emily Delbridge (Twines) is an interdisciplinary performance and technology artist, writer, and software engineer with a passion for storytelling through new and challenging media. She has experience directing, writing, programming, and designing interactive theatrical elements in NYC, where she earned her MA in Theatre History and Criticism as well as her MFA in Performance and Interactive Media Arts.

Works include writing for "The House," a short film by renowned director Cyril Teste and team, as well as work on "I heard an echo," a collaborative installation at The Weeksville Heritage Center in Brooklyn, for which she provided video and interactivity elements through use of Max MSP, a Kinect, and an Arduino set-up.

Her COVID project, a narrative, video-art-driven video game called LookingGlass, was streamed and played via The Living Gallery, OyeDrum literary magazine, ReclaimFutures' festival, and EdgeCut.

In 2025, she debuted a short animated film, "Anthelion," at the Wavestock Film Festival on Staten Island, and subsequently completed a sibling animated story, "The Well," which uses the form of a parable to explore grief and the power of community to heal.

She has recently re-discovered her childhood passion for narrative writing, working on pieces ranging from picture books to allegorical stories, all with a particular interest in exploring the full range of human emotion.

Whatever the project and whichever the media, she creates according to a simple credo: to offer opportunities for connection and little shared moments of hope.
